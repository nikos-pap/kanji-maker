A few simple tools

Enter a character and load it. Erase removes parts of its shape; Cut area removes a rectangle. Draw and Line add strokes. Use Undo whenever you need it.

Scroll over the canvas to zoom around the pointer, or use the zoom buttons. Hold Space and drag, use the middle mouse button, or choose Pan to move around. Fit shows the whole character. Pinch with two fingers on a touch screen.

Points edits the original font outline. Click a square point to select it; circles are curve handles. Drag to reshape, enter exact X/Y coordinates, or use arrow keys to move by 1 unit (Shift: 10, Alt: 0.1). Hold Shift while dragging to constrain movement horizontally or vertically. Drag a highlighted outline to move that entire connected shape. Remove outline deletes the selected contour; connected strokes may belong to the same contour.

Polygon cut removes an area with precise straight edges: click its corners, then Apply cut, Enter, or double-click to finish. Escape cancels an unfinished cut.

Hold Shift while drawing a line to snap its angle. Shortcuts: N, E, R, P, D, L, H switch tools; 0 fits; Ctrl+Z undoes; Ctrl+Shift+Z redoes.

Save characters to your library, then export the whole library as a Word document with its font embedded, one reusable font file, or an editable app file. Each saved character has its own code; adding or deleting entries does not change existing codes. Importing into an empty library restores the original font identity and codes; importing into an existing library adds copies with fresh codes.

Copy for document can copy formatted editable text or a transparent image. Text copies use the library font: install it once in the receiving application, and update it after adding or editing characters. Keep source formatting when pasting into Word. Images need no custom font, but behave as pictures. The Word document export embeds the font automatically.

Individual SVG, PNG, font and app-file exports remain available for the current character. Its separate single-character font uses U+E000. SVG keeps source curves and transparent cuts; fonts preserve native quadratic outlines where possible and trace composed cuts/strokes at high resolution.

The included source font is derived from Noto Serif CJK, available under the SIL Open Font License. Its licence is included with font downloads.
